Self-Tape & Audition Tips

Everything you need to know — from the moment your agent calls to pressing send on your tape.

Auditions lead to work. And even when they don't, making a good impression in front of a casting director makes all the difference. This guide will help you prepare, perform, and present yourself at your best — every time.

📞
Step One

Your Agent Just Called… Now What?

→

Confirm as soon as possible. If you have conflicts, call, email or text your agent right away. Deadlines are extremely tight and it is never good to make a casting director wait.

→

Last-minute auditions are part of the job. Do not be thrown off. Review the instructions carefully the moment they come in.

→

Read the material in a calm state of mind. Don't place too much pressure on yourself — the process is more enjoyable and more successful that way.

→

Don't leave it to the last minute. Casting directors are on your side — but they can't ask for a reshoot if you send it too close to the deadline.

🎬
Step Two

Before the Shoot

→

Research the brand or company. Understand their style choices from previous projects and get a sense of what they're looking for.

→

Do breathing exercises and be as comfortable as possible before taping.

→

Practice your part multiple times before recording. By the time you press record, you should be able to act as naturally as possible.

📷
Step Three

How to Set Up Your Shot

→

Pick a clean, simple background. They're looking for your performance, not set design. A blank background works best.

→

Test your lighting first. Natural lighting is best. Make sure you can be heard clearly through the video.

→

Never place the camera below your eye level. Raise your phone or laptop using books. Don't stand too far away. Frame yourself from chest to top of head — a medium close-up.

👗
Step Four

What to Wear

Dress as close to the character as possible — but not eccentrically. Keep it plain, without patterns.

Wear something flattering but neutral. Minimal jewellery only. No big headphones — they're distracting.

Make-up: less is more. Keep it subtle and natural. Be well groomed.

It's a balancing act — be your version of the character. Real and relatable always wins.

🎥
Step Five

Start Taping!

→

Start with an ident. State your name, agent/agency, and the role. Take a full-length shot and look straight into the camera during the ident only.

→

Don't look into the camera while acting. If nervous, you can mention in the ident that it's your first audition — that's completely fine.

→

Stick to the script. For commercials especially — it's about being real and relatable. Show the best version of you, not perfection.

→

Be you. Do your personal take but don't overdo it. Keep it simple but effective.

📤
Step Six

Sending Your Tape & What Comes Next

→

Send to your agent. Be willing to receive notes and pivot — don't be attached to a certain version. Be open to criticism and willing to retake.

→

Once sent — relax. Forget about it. Don't dwell on what you could have done differently.

If you don't get the job — it's not personal. It can come down to accent, beard length, size of teeth… anything. Castings are a marathon, not a sprint. Your role will come.

⭐
Always Improving

In the Meantime — Improve Your Chances

→

Communicate with your agent. Always update them if your availability changes or you're going away. It shows professionalism.

→

Update your measurements regularly and keep them on a PDF. Every casting asks for them — at submission, recall, and contract stage.

→

Polish your CV with skills that help you stand out — accents, languages, instruments, dancing, singing, martial arts, gymnastics, combat, athletics. Whatever you list, you must do it well. Send skill videos to your agent.

Ready to Get Started?

It's time to start taping. Good luck — we're rooting for you.
